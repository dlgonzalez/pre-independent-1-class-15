Challenge 1 · 10 min
The Number Vault

What comes next?

3 → 6 → 12 → 24 → ?

Talk to your team: what is changing from one number to the next? Choose the missing number.

Look at how each number changes. Is it +3, +6, or ×2 every time?
Say it: “I think the answer is… because…” · “Can you explain your reasoning?” · “Let's check the pattern.”
Challenge 2 · 10 min
The Mystery Object

Who am I?

I have keys but no locks. I have a space but no room. You can enter, but you cannot walk inside. What am I?

Discuss the clues. Choose the object that fits all three.

Think of an object with a SPACE key and an ENTER key.
Say it: “It could be… because…” · “But does it have…?” · “All the clues fit!”
Challenge 3 · 12 min
The Fake News Trap

Which action is the smartest?

Your friend sends a dramatic post: “BREAKING! Schools will close for a month tomorrow!” It has no source and asks everyone to share immediately.

📲 “Share this NOW before they delete it!”

Your team must choose the best first action.

Likes and urgency are not proof. Look for evidence from reliable, independent sources.
Say it: “We shouldn't rely on…” · “We need to find out whether…” · “Our evidence would be…”
Challenge 4 · 13 min
The Last Door · Logic Puzzle

Who has the access card?

Three teammates—Alex, Bea and Chris—each have one different object: a notebook, a flashlight, or the access card.

🧩 Clue 1: Alex has the notebook.

🧩 Clue 2: Bea does not have the flashlight.

🧩 Clue 3: Each person has exactly one object.

Who has the access card? Discuss how you know.

Alex already has the notebook. Bea has either the flashlight or the card—but Clue 2 rules out the flashlight.
Say it: “Alex can't have it because…” · “That means Bea must have…” · “We can eliminate…”
Final lock

Enter the four-digit code 🔓

When your team has solved all four challenges, enter the digits in the order you earned them.

Mission accomplished? You've practised patterns, lateral thinking, evidence checking and logical reasoning. Those skills are useful beyond puzzles. Now it's time to apply them to a real-life challenge: social media.
02

Social Media Detectives

15 minutes · Reading and discussion · Coursebook page 34

In the escape room, you learned not to trust a dramatic post without checking it. Now read “The Trouble With Social Media” on page 34. Your task is to recognise common problems and choose one your team wants to solve.
Read and investigate

Find the problem in each situation

Read the article in your coursebook. Then solve these five mini-cases. After each answer, read the feedback to understand why it fits.

Case 1: Mateo only follows people who agree with him and believes everything they share.

Case 2: Sara checks her phone constantly and stops paying attention to her friends.

Case 3: People post cruel comments about a classmate in a group chat.

Case 4: An employer sees an old public post that someone forgot about.

Case 5: A person shares their location and announces that their home will be empty.

Discuss and decide

Which problem should your team solve?

Talk in your breakout room. Choose one problem, explain why it matters, and suggest how an app might help. You can discuss fictional situations; you do not need to share private experiences.

Useful language: “The biggest problem is…” · “It affects people because…” · “I see your point, but…” · “What if an app could…?”
You have identified a real problem. Your next mission is to create a useful app. A good solution needs more than a cool name: it needs clear users, a practical purpose, and a simple explanation of how it works.
03

Design Your App · Starbursting Studio

25 minutes · Teamwork and creative problem-solving

Imagine you work for a company. Your manager asks you to come up with an app that helps people deal with social media problems. First, share your ideas. Then choose one and improve it by asking six types of questions: Who, What, Where, When, How and Why.
Step 1 · Create the concept · 5 min

Your app identity

Each teammate suggests an idea. Choose one together. Your app can fact-check stories, protect privacy, reduce screen time, or help users respond to cyberbullying.

Step 2 · Starbursting · 12 min

Six questions to make your idea stronger

Ask and answer the questions with your group. Short, clear answers are fine. If an answer is unclear, ask a follow-up question.

👥 WHO?

Who needs this app?

❓ WHAT?

What does it do?

📍 WHERE?

Where can people use it?

🕒 WHEN?

When would people need it?

⚙️ HOW?

How does it work?

💡 WHY?

Why is it useful?

Keep the conversation going: “What else could it do?” · “Can you give an example?” · “I like that idea because…” · “What if we added…?”
Step 3 · Improve and prepare · 8 min

Is your idea ready?

Your app has a purpose, users, and a design. Now it's time to pitch it! Explain your idea clearly, give a reason for your choices, and be ready for questions from your classmates.
04

Final Pitch · The App Showcase

20 minutes · Speaking production and Q&A

Welcome to the final showcase! You are presenting your app to an audience. Your goal is not to use complicated English; your goal is to make your classmates understand the problem, your solution, and why it matters.
Your team pitch

Present in about 2 minutes

  1. The problem: What problem are you solving?
  2. The name: What is your app called?
  3. The users: Who is it for?
  4. The function: How does it work?
  5. The benefit: Why would people use it?
Example opening

“Have you ever seen a story online and wondered if it was true? Our app, FactFriend, helps users check suspicious stories before they share them.”

Audience mission

Listen, question, and respond

After each presentation, ask a question that helps the team explain its idea more clearly.

“Who is your app designed for?”
“What happens when someone uses it?”
“Why is this better than doing nothing?”
“What could you improve in the future?”

Answer politely: “That's a good question.” · “Our idea is to…” · “We haven't decided yet, but we could…”

Quick speaking self-check

You've completed your final team project. Before you finish Pre-Independent 1, reflect on what you've learned and how your communication has grown.
